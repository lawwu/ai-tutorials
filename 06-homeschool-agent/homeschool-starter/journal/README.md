# journal/ — what actually happened

One file per day: `journal/YYYY/YYYY-MM-DD.md`, organized by child.

**This folder is append-only.** Never edit or delete a past entry. What
happened on a Tuesday in October should not be quietly rewritten in March —
the value of a journal is that it's a record, not a summary.

## How entries get created

You don't write these by hand. Talk into your phone, paste the transcript,
and let the assistant file it:

> log: math was a fight this morning, she asked for two breaks. Finished
> 4A chapter 1 lesson 3 anyway. The little one did four pages of PKB and
> asked why the moon is out during the day — I owe her an answer.

From that one dump the assistant should: write today's journal entry,
update the math position in `curricula/`, and add the unanswered question
to `TODO.md`.

## What a filed entry looks like

```markdown
# 2026-08-13

## CHILD_ONE
- Math 10:00-11:00. Dimensions 4A ch 1 lesson 3. Two breaks requested
  (10:20, 10:40). Stopped before the third page rather than push.
- Read independently for 30 min after lunch.

## CHILD_TWO
- PKB, 4 pages.
- Asked why the moon is visible during the day — unanswered, see TODO.

## Notes
- Mom's read: the tiredness was downstream of not understanding the
  lesson, not the cause.
```

## Why this pays off

Six months in, the journal is the only honest answer to "how is she
actually doing?" — better than any plan, because plans record intentions
and journals record what happened. Ask:

> Read the last two months of journal entries. What patterns do you see in
> how math is going?
